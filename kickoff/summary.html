📌Key decisions and agreements
1.Year 1 deliverable. The group builds and pilots a framework for deciding whether a proposed topic belongs in the Mount Sinai MD curriculum. No new curriculum content is developed in Year 1. The framework goes to the EEC around July 2027; Year 2 applies it to a broader set of topics.
2.Framework shape. Six steps: intake, gap test, evidence packet, expert input, scoring, and decision. Four possible decisions: integrate, pilot, monitor, or watchful waiting (not now).
3.Two-question rule. A topic that LCME, the MEPOs, the AAMC, USMLE/NBME or another national body already expects goes to the Curriculum Gap Working Group (G2.2). G3.1 evaluates topics with no clear requirement. Topics handed to G2.2 still carry their evidence from this group.
4.Evidence bar. A topic does not have to meet all 11 framework criteria, but a position statement or external list alone is not enough. The framework includes a stress test, guidance on how to integrate, and trigger indicators for when a monitored topic should be acted on.
5.Pilot. The framework is tested on three to five topics the group agrees on. Topics close to an existing requirement are good test cases. AI was named as an example, not yet selected.
6.One place for proposals. A shared SharePoint spreadsheet will hold every proposed topic and its evidence. This dashboard is the single place for the group's links.
7.Sequence. The charter is approved first; the December meeting is scheduled after that.
📝Discussion summary

Why a strategic plan now

The MD program's plan, From Education to Impact, aligns with the institution-wide strategic plan. LCME Element 1.1 (strategic planning) anchors the other standards, and the plan, its goals, milestones and progress data need to be in place for the October 2027 LCME visit. There are 12 Year 1 working groups. Rainier Soriano and Ravi Ramaswamy co-lead G3.1 and G2.2; Kevin Han is project manager for G3.1 and the overall plan.

What counts as an emerging topic

Knowledge, a capability or a skill that is changing and that graduates will need over the next five to ten years, which the MD program is not yet expected to teach. Internal factors, external factors and the school's mission all bear on the decision. Novelty or Mount Sinai expertise alone does not place a topic in the core curriculum; some topics may suit subspecialty training better.

Evidence sources reviewed

LCME Standards 6 and 7 (Standard 7 now names specific topics); the 23 MEPOs; AAMC Core EPAs; USMLE and NBME exams (Phase 1 NBME CAS exams, Phase 2 shelf exams, CBSE and CCSE); state and federal mandates; GME expectations; the AAMC Curriculum SCOPE Survey (SCOPE survey page on this dashboard); and stakeholder proposals. The program already holds data for most of these: Step performance, clerkship evaluations, the Graduation Questionnaire and clinical-experience logs.

Starting topic list (from published sources)

AI and digital health; climate change and health; genomics beyond precision-medicine basics (named as an institutional priority); firearm injury prevention; telehealth (most schools kept it after the pandemic; the program has little now); disability health; occupational health; complementary medicine; veterans' care; medical humanities. Nutrition is tied to state and federal mandates and so is likely a G2.2 topic.
