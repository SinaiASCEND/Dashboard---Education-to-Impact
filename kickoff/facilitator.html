Slide 4 of 31
Say

Before introductions, a working definition, so we all mean the same thing. An emerging healthcare topic is knowledge or a capability that is changing, or likely to change, how our graduates practice in the next five to ten years, and that the program is not yet expected to teach by accreditation, our own program objectives, the AAMC Core EPAs, the licensing and subject exams, or what most schools require. What it is not: a topic we are already expected to teach, even if we teach it too little; that is a gap for G2.2. And a topic does not qualify just because someone champions it; it still needs a source and a reason. Agreeing this definition is part of today's decision, so tell us now or at the wrap-up if the wording should change.

TIPPart of today's decision (purpose and scope, milestone Nov 4). Record any change to the wording.

Slide 7 of 31
Say

This is the shape of what we build this year. Every proposed topic goes through the same six steps. Anyone can propose a topic. The gap test sends anything the program is already expected to teach to G2.2. Each remaining topic gets a one-page evidence packet across eleven criteria, each supplied by the people best placed to answer it. Members score privately first, then discuss only where scores differ. The result is one of four decisions: integrate, pilot, monitor or not now. And every adopted topic gets outcome measures and a review date. Two questions this forces that usually get skipped: what comes out to make room, and how will we know it worked. We set the scoring and decision rules together by February 18.

TIPAbout 2 minutes. The criteria are drafted in December and January; today is the picture only.

⚖II. Gap or Emerging, and Where the Evidence Comes From3:07–3:12
Slide 8 of 31
Say

Before we look at topics, we need one shared test. If accreditation, our own program objectives (the MEPOs), the AAMC Core EPAs, the USMLE or NBME exams, or the national norm (AAMC SCOPE) already expects a topic, it is a curriculum gap. It goes to the Curriculum Gap Working Group, G2.2, whether or not we teach it now. Only what lies beyond those expectations is an emerging topic for this group. Two-part topics split.

What members see under this slide

If accreditation (LCME), our own program objectives (MEPOs), the AAMC Core EPAs, the USMLE or NBME exams, or the national norm (AAMC SCOPE: what most schools require) already expects a topic, it is a curriculum gap and goes to G2.2, whether or not we teach it now. Only what lies beyond those expectations is an emerging topic for this group.

Slide 9 of 31
Say

These sources answer the first question of the rule: is the program expected to teach this? Some are requirements: accreditation, LCME Standards 6 and 7; our own program objectives, the MEPOs, which Standard 6 makes binding; decisions our EEC has made, such as the societal problems we chose; and state and federal law. Others set a strong expectation: what the USMLE and NBME exams test, the AAMC Core EPAs for entering residency, what residency programs count on, and the national norm from SCOPE, the percent of schools that require a topic. SCOPE is a norm, so we still need a threshold for how many schools is enough. Any of these makes a topic expected: a G2.2 gap if we do not teach it. The next slides give the strengths and limits of each.

Slide 17 of 31
Say

You may not know how much we can already check. If someone proposes a topic, we can see whether the USMLE or the subject exams test it and how our students do there, compared with students nationally; whether and where we teach it, for how many hours, and against which objectives; what our students and graduates say about their preparation; how many schools require it; and whether students actually see the condition or do the procedure in their clinical years. These can support a topic, or show it is already covered. All of it is aggregate, and the Office of Curricular Affairs pulls it. Anything new, such as a survey, goes through the Data Request and Survey Approval form.

TIPPoint out data members may not know we have, such as USMLE results by content area and the curriculum map's USMLE crosswalk.
