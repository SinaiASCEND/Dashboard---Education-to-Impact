Slide 4 of 37
Say

Before introductions, the definition we work from, so we all mean the same thing. A curriculum gap is content the program is expected to teach, by accreditation, our own program objectives, an EEC decision, law, the licensing exams, the Core EPAs or what most schools require, that we teach too little or not at all, do not assess, or that students do not experience or do not master. What it is not: a topic beyond what the standards expect; that goes to the Emerging Topics group, G3.1. And content that is taught but simply not tagged on the curriculum map is a mapping issue: we fix the map, not the curriculum. This definition is in the charter; if the wording does not fit your area, tell us now or at the wrap-up.

TIPIf a member asks to change the wording, record it; wording changes go through the Change Log.
What members see under this slide
Definition

A curriculum gap is content the MD program is expected to teach, by accreditation (LCME), our own program objectives (MEPOs), an EEC decision, law, the licensing and subject exams (USMLE, NBME), the AAMC Core EPAs or the national norm (AAMC SCOPE), that we teach too little or not at all, do not assess, or that our students do not experience or do not master.

It is not a topic beyond what the standards expect (that is a G3.1 emerging topic), and not content that is taught but untagged on the map (a mapping issue). The definition is in the charter, Appendix A.

Slide 7 of 37
Say

This is the path every gap takes this year, in nine steps. One: it is identified and gets a register row with its source. Two: the gap test, whether we are expected to teach it and how much we teach now. Three: its type. Four and five: its owner gathers two kinds of evidence, and we judge how confident we are; a mapping issue goes to the map team, not to the EEC. Six: a tier, a USMLE tag and the subcommittee that leads it. Seven and eight: the owner drafts a recommendation, the subcommittee reviews it, and the EEC decides Address, Defer or Monitor. Nine: Address gaps get an owner, a plan and a target term, approved with the decision; round 1 plans are final by March 31, when building starts, and each gap is closed by its target term. The Review steps tab on the dashboard has every step in detail. Our four key performance indicators follow that path: the decisions, the curriculum map as the evidence base, the Annual Curriculum Gap Report, and closing the Address gaps.

The same rule the Emerging Topics group uses, from our side. First question: is the program expected to teach this? If accreditation, our MEPOs, an EEC decision, the law, the USMLE or NBME exams, the Core EPAs or most schools expect it, it belongs here, whether or not we teach it now. Second question: how much do we teach? Enough, and it comes off the register. Too little, and it is a gap. Nothing at all, and it is a larger gap, not an emerging topic; rehabilitative care is the example. Two-part topics split: basic AI use is our gap; AI beyond the basics goes to G3.1.

Say

Accreditation first. LCME Standards 6 and 7 are binding: our accreditation depends on them, and they apply to every US MD program, so they set a clear floor. Changes are announced ahead of time, as with the 2027–28 standards. The limits: they name broad areas, rarely specific topics, and they set a minimum, not best practice. For us they matter most because the topics Standard 7 names set our Tier 1 and Tier 2; the five societal problems our EEC chose under 7.5 are tagged on the map only in Phase 1.

Say

The AAMC Core EPAs: thirteen tasks every graduate should be able to do on the first day of residency. They are national, concrete and easy to map to teaching and assessment, and they link medical school to residency. The limits: they are not an accreditation requirement, they describe tasks rather than content, and a topic may fit several EPAs or none. Procedural skills on our register sit under EPA 12.

Slide 13 of 37
Say

The USMLE and NBME exams: the Step 1 and Step 2 CK outlines, and the shelf and CAS exams our students sit. They link directly to student outcomes, they are specific and kept up to date, and their weights show relative emphasis. The limits: they test knowledge, not skills; they lag new practice; and teaching to the exam can crowd out other aims. For us they also set the route: Step 1 content goes to PCCS, Step 2 CK to CCS. State and federal mandates: laws tied to licensure or prescribing. They are not optional for our graduates' practice, and content and hours are usually spelled out. AAMC SCOPE: for each topic, the percent of schools that require it. It is national, comparable and repeated each year. The limits: it is self-reported, a required topic can mean a single session, figures come from different survey years, and it is a norm, not a requirement. Slide 17 of 37
Say

Each gap ends up in one of four places. Confirmed, when two kinds agree. Possible, when we have one kind only, and the group decides what to check next. Mapping issue, when the map shows a gap but other evidence shows the content is taught or experienced; the fix is a map correction. And not supported, when the evidence does not show a gap. We use student data only as aggregates, and evaluation comments only as counted themes, never verbatim.

What members see under this slide

Confirmed, possible, mapping issue or not supported. Every register row carries its evidence by kind, its confidence, its USMLE tag and an owner. Student data are used only as aggregates, and evaluation comments only as counted themes.

Slide 27 of 37
Say

These are the three decisions the EEC can make, with proposed rules for which one we recommend; we ask the group to confirm them today. Address when the gap is confirmed and on Tier 1 or Tier 2, or when a confirmed Monitor gap shows our students below national or missing a required clinical experience; it needs an owner, a plan and a target term, approved with the decision and final by March 31 for round 1. Defer when the gap is confirmed but closing it depends on something already scheduled, such as the Nutrition Content Area Director's appointment; it needs a reason and a return date. Monitor when we have one kind of evidence only, a national norm only, or outcomes at or above national; it names what to watch and a review date. Mapping issues and unsupported gaps need no decision: a map correction, or removal with the reason.
