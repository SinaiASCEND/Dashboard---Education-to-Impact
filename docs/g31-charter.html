Purpose

Emerging-healthcare teaching exists but is scattered: it depends on individual faculty champions and has no shared framework. AI in medicine appears in four Phase 1 sessions in the AY 2026–27 curriculum map, one of them a selective, and the open comment period raised the pacing of AI content in the curriculum.

The Steering Committee kept this initiative at Meeting 5 with two caveats: whether to reach beyond AI and digital health, and the need for an instructional designer to turn best-effort teaching into an organized framework. Year 1 drafts that framework from what is already taught; it does not add or launch new content.

Scope for Year 1

Decision to make first

A core of AI and digital health, with scientific innovation, research, entrepreneurship and MICCA as named optional domains the framework can add in Year 2. This draft recommends that option: it meets the January 2027 draft deadline, and keeps the broader domains on the map rather than dropping them.

Gap or emerging topic?

Each topic has one home. The test: does someone already expect the MD program to teach it?

Gap (G2.2)Emerging topic (G3.1)
TestAn expectation already exists and the curriculum falls shortPractice is moving ahead of any settled expectation
EvidenceLCME Standard 7, MEPOs, the USMLE content outline, outcome data, or most schools already requiring it (AAMC SCOPE)Horizon scans, Mount Sinai institutional strengths, few schools teaching it yet
AimCatch up to the floorGet ahead, then set the bar
OutputAn EEC decision: Address, Defer or MonitorA competency framework

A topic moves from emerging to gap once a standard names it: the 2027–28 Standard 7 names artificial intelligence, so its basic, required use is a G2.2 gap while its frontier stays in G3.1. Nutrition moved the same way when USMLE added it to every Step in June 2026.

Ways of working

One standing 90-minute slot serves both working groups: G2.2 in the first half, G3.1 in the second.

  • First meeting. The week of Nov 2, 2026; the project managers are setting the day and time.
  • Cadence. Monthly; every two weeks in November while the inventory and structure are built.
  • First-meeting agenda. Confirm the emerging topics with the gap test, decide scope, assign the inventory by topic.
  • Decisions. The co-leads decide the draft; the EEC reviews the framework.
  • Reporting. Monthly status report to Kevin Han from December; quarterly report-outs; tasks in Jira.
  • Records. Minutes in OneDrive under Strategic Plan > Minutes > G3.1.
