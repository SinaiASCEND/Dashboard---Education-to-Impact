2. Scope

Emerging healthcare topics reach the curriculum through individual faculty champions, with no shared test of whether a topic belongs. AI in medicine appears in four Phase 1 sessions in the AY 2026–27 curriculum map, one of them a selective, and the open comment period raised the pacing of AI content.

The Steering Committee kept this initiative at Meeting 5 with two caveats: whether to reach beyond AI and digital health, and the need for an instructional designer. Year 1 builds an evaluative framework, a needs assessment based on Kern's six-step approach to curriculum development and LCME Element 8.1, that decides whether an emerging topic belongs in the MD curriculum, and how. Year 1 adds no new content; an instructional designer is needed from Year 2, when topics are built into the curriculum.

Appendix A. Gap or emerging topic?

Whether a topic is already taught does not decide G2.2 or G3.1; whether someone expects it does. Ask two questions, in order.

  1. Is the program expected to teach it? It is expected if LCME Standard 7, USMLE or the national norm (most schools require it, per AAMC SCOPE) names it. Yes: G2.2. No: G3.1.
  2. For an expected topic, how much do we teach now? This decides whether there is a gap and how large it is, inside G2.2.
What we teach of an expected topicResult
EnoughNot a gap; it drops off the register
Too littleG2.2 gap
NothingG2.2 gap, and a larger one (rehabilitative care, for example)

An expected topic with nothing in the curriculum is the most urgent kind of G2.2 gap, not an emerging topic. The one exception is a topic with two parts: the expected part goes to G2.2 and the part beyond it to G3.1. Basic AI use (named in the 2027–28 standards) is a G2.2 gap while frontier AI goes to G3.1; genomics splits the same way.

Appendix B. Evaluative framework: draft criteria

Based on the needs-assessment steps of Kern's six-step approach to curriculum development (problem identification and targeted needs assessment), with its evaluation step at the end, and on LCME Element 8.1 (the curriculum committee controls what enters the curriculum). Each topic is scored on the criteria, and the scores lead to one of the decisions below.
