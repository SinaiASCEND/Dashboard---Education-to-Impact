Purpose

Known content gaps have no single owner and no documented decision, so they persist from one review cycle to the next. The AY 2025–26 curriculum gap analysis, updated with October 2026 data, found, among others:

  • LCME Standard 7 topics that need teaching built or strengthened: rehabilitative care has no dedicated session, and the five societal problems the EEC approved for Element 7.5 are tagged on the map only in Phase 1
  • Healthcare Delivery Science is the thinnest Area of Curricular Concentration in Phase 1: 10 of its 27 sessions include Phase 1
  • Healthcare economics at 2 Phase 1 sessions and resource-limited care at none; AI in medicine and point-of-care ultrasound at 4 to 5 sessions each, with no longitudinal thread
  • Nutrition at 8 dedicated Phase 1 sessions (about 7.5 hours) against a 40-hour benchmark; none of the 36 JAMA nutrition competencies fully covered. The 2027–28 Standard 7 names nutrition, and USMLE added it to every Step from June 2026
  • 34 of 93 required clinical experiences untagged in Phase 1; six program objectives (MEPOs 6, 7, 12, 14, 17, 18) reinforced only in Phase 1
  • Clerkship shelf exams for the Class of 2028 at or above the national mean in OB/GYN and Pediatrics; Emergency Medicine 3.5 points below

The open comment period (Aug 17 – Sept 10) routed two more concerns here: the basic-science teaching model and the pacing of AI content. This initiative gives each gap an owner, evidence, a priority and a governance decision, works in priority order with LCME Standard 7 topics first, and builds the reporting that keeps gaps visible after Year 1.

Gap or emerging topic?

Each topic has one home. The test: does someone already expect the MD program to teach it?

Gap (G2.2)Emerging topic (G3.1)
TestAn expectation already exists and the curriculum falls shortPractice is moving ahead of any settled expectation
EvidenceLCME Standard 7, MEPOs, the USMLE content outline, outcome data, or most schools already requiring it (AAMC SCOPE)Horizon scans, Mount Sinai institutional strengths, few schools teaching it yet
AimCatch up to the floorGet ahead, then set the bar
OutputAn EEC decision: Address, Defer or MonitorA competency framework

A topic moves from emerging to gap once a standard names it: the 2027–28 Standard 7 names artificial intelligence, so its basic, required use is a G2.2 gap while its frontier stays in G3.1. Nutrition moved the same way when USMLE added it to every Step in June 2026.

Ways of working

One standing 90-minute slot serves both working groups: G2.2 in the first half, G3.1 in the second. The co-leads are the same and several members overlap.

  • First meeting. The week of Nov 2, 2026; the project managers are setting the day and time.
  • Cadence. Every two weeks through December, then monthly.
  • First-meeting agenda. Confirm the gap list and its priority order (LCME Standard 7 topics first), give each gap a content owner and a route, agree the Address, Defer and Monitor criteria for the charter.
  • Decisions. The co-leads decide the group's recommendations; the EEC decides each gap.
  • Reporting. Monthly status report to Kevin Han from December; quarterly report-outs; tasks in Jira.
  • Records. Minutes in OneDrive under Strategic Plan > Minutes > G2.2; new data requests through the Data Request & Survey Approval form.
