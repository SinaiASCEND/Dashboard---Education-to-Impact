2. Scope

Curriculum content gaps have no single owner and no documented decision, and they carry over from one review cycle to the next. The AY 2025–26 curriculum gap analysis, updated with October 2026 data, identified the following gaps, among others:

  • LCME Standard 7 topics that need teaching built or strengthened: rehabilitative care has no dedicated session, and the five societal problems the EEC approved for Element 7.5 are tagged on the map only in Phase 1
  • Nutrition at 8 dedicated Phase 1 sessions (about 7.5 hours) against a 40-hour benchmark; none of the 36 JAMA nutrition competencies fully covered. The 2027–28 Standard 7 names nutrition, and USMLE added it to every Step from June 2026
  • Healthcare Delivery Science has the lowest Phase 1 presence of any Area of Curricular Concentration: 10 of its 27 sessions include Phase 1
  • Healthcare economics at 2 Phase 1 sessions and resource-limited care at none; AI in medicine at 4 sessions, with no longitudinal thread
  • 34 of 93 required clinical experiences untagged in Phase 1; six program objectives (MEPOs 6, 7, 12, 14, 17, 18) reinforced only in Phase 1
  • Class of 2028 clerkship shelf exams: Emergency Medicine 3.5 points below the national mean; OB/GYN and Pediatrics at or above it

Two themes from the open comment period (Aug 17 – Sept 10) are referred to this initiative: the basic-science teaching model and the pacing of AI content. In Year 1, the initiative assigns each gap an owner, evidence, a priority tier, a USMLE tag, a review route and an EEC decision. It also produces the Annual Curriculum Gap Report, which tracks gaps in every year after Year 1.

8. Cadence and venue

The two working groups meet separately, monthly.

  • Meetings. Meeting 1 (kickoff) Nov 12; then the second Monday of each month at 11 AM: Dec 14, Jan 11, Feb 8, Mar 8, Apr 12 and May 10.
  • First-meeting agenda. Confirm the gap register, owners, tiers and routes.
  • Decisions. The working group recommends directly to the EEC, with an FYI to PCCS, CCS and CIS (their approval is not required); the EEC decides each gap; the subcommittees then own the actions, with ongoing CQI monitoring.
  • Reviews. Reviewers gather data with the data-gathering template, starting from the curriculum map data on where each topic is taught and assessed, and present findings with a standard template; the group agrees each topic's status, as in the LCME CQI process.
  • Reporting. Monthly status report to Kevin Han from December; quarterly report-outs; tasks in Jira.
  • Records. Minutes posted on the strategic plan dashboard and the working group's SharePoint (03_Working_Groups > P2_G2.2_Curriculum-Gap-Closure), kept by the project manager; new data requests through the Data Request & Survey Approval form.

Venue: Zoom or a room set by the project manager, in the Monday Curricular Affairs meeting window (10:00–12:00).

Appendix A. Gap or emerging topic: the assignment rule

Definition. An emerging healthcare topic is knowledge or a capability that is changing, or is likely to change, how graduates practice in the next five to ten years, and that the MD program is not yet expected to teach under accreditation (LCME), the program objectives (MEPOs), the AAMC Core EPAs, the licensing and subject exams (USMLE, NBME) or the national norm (AAMC SCOPE). A topic is assigned to G2.2 or G3.1 by whether the program is expected to teach it, not by whether it is already taught. Two questions are applied in order.

  1. Expectation. A topic is expected if LCME Standards 6 and 7, the MEPOs, an EEC decision, state or federal law, the USMLE or NBME exams, the AAMC Core EPAs or the national norm (required by most schools, per AAMC SCOPE) calls for it. Expected topics go to G2.2; all others go to G3.1.
  2. Current coverage. For an expected topic, the amount taught now determines whether a G2.2 gap exists and how large it is.
What we teach of an expected topicResult
EnoughNot a gap; removed from the register
Too littleG2.2 gap
NothingG2.2 gap of larger size (for example, rehabilitative care)

An expected topic with no coverage in the curriculum is a G2.2 gap of the highest urgency, not an emerging topic. A topic with an expected part and a part beyond it is split: the expected part goes to G2.2 and the remainder to G3.1. Basic AI use, named in the 2027–28 standards, is a G2.2 gap, and AI beyond the basics is a G3.1 topic; genomics is split the same way.

Appendix C. USMLE alignment and routing

USMLE alignment rule. Content known to be aligned with USMLE Step 1 is prioritized and stays with PCCS. Content known or potentially aligned with Step 2 CK goes to CCS. A gap's Step alignment is set from the USMLE content crosswalk of the curriculum map and the current USMLE content outlines.

Routing order:

  1. A gap that falls under one of the four Areas of Curricular Concentration (AOCs: SSD, HDS, PCA, LPI) goes to CIS instead of PCCS or CCS. For every other gap, the USMLE rule decides between PCCS and CCS for content taught in modules and clerkships; when a gap is on both Steps, PCCS leads and CCS covers the clinical application.
  2. The Clinical Competency group (the Director of Clinical Competency, the POM directors and the Clinical Competency Mentors) takes clinical-skills gaps: communication, procedures, POM and PEAKS.
  3. CIS also takes gaps in thread structure (for example, the AI and nutrition threads), and may be consulted on others.
  4. The working group recommends directly to the EEC, with an FYI to the subcommittee on the route, whose approval is not required; the route sets which subcommittee owns the resulting actions. The EEC decides every Tier 1 and Tier 2 gap.

USMLE tags are from the 2026 Step 1 and Step 2 CK content outlines and the Oct 16 crosswalk.
