2. Scope

Known content gaps have no single owner and no documented decision, so they persist from one review cycle to the next. The AY 2025–26 curriculum gap analysis, updated with October 2026 data, found, among others:

  • LCME Standard 7 topics that need teaching built or strengthened: rehabilitative care has no dedicated session, and the five societal problems the EEC approved for Element 7.5 are tagged on the map only in Phase 1
  • Nutrition at 8 dedicated Phase 1 sessions (about 7.5 hours) against a 40-hour benchmark; none of the 36 JAMA nutrition competencies fully covered. The 2027–28 Standard 7 names nutrition, and USMLE added it to every Step from June 2026
  • Healthcare Delivery Science is the thinnest Area of Curricular Concentration in Phase 1: 10 of its 27 sessions include Phase 1
  • Healthcare economics at 2 Phase 1 sessions and resource-limited care at none; AI in medicine at 4 sessions, with no longitudinal thread
  • 34 of 93 required clinical experiences untagged in Phase 1; six program objectives (MEPOs 6, 7, 12, 14, 17, 18) reinforced only in Phase 1
  • Clerkship shelf exams for the Class of 2028 at or above the national mean in OB/GYN and Pediatrics; Emergency Medicine 3.5 points below

The open comment period (Aug 17 – Sept 10) routed two more concerns here: the basic-science teaching model and the pacing of AI content. This initiative gives each gap an owner, evidence, a tier, a USMLE tag, a route and an EEC decision, and builds the Annual Curriculum Gap Report that keeps gaps visible after Year 1.

Appendix A. Gap or emerging topic?

Whether a topic is already taught does not decide G2.2 or G3.1; whether someone expects it does. Ask two questions, in order.

  1. Is the program expected to teach it? It is expected if LCME Standard 7, USMLE or the national norm (most schools require it, per AAMC SCOPE) names it. Yes: G2.2. No: G3.1.
  2. For an expected topic, how much do we teach now? This decides whether there is a gap and how large it is, inside G2.2.
What we teach of an expected topicResult
EnoughNot a gap; it drops off the register
Too littleG2.2 gap
NothingG2.2 gap, and a larger one (rehabilitative care, for example)

An expected topic with nothing in the curriculum is the most urgent kind of G2.2 gap, not an emerging topic. The one exception is a topic with two parts: the expected part goes to G2.2 and the part beyond it to G3.1. Basic AI use (named in the 2027–28 standards) is a G2.2 gap while frontier AI goes to G3.1; genomics splits the same way.

Appendix C. USMLE alignment and routing

USMLE alignment rule. Content known to be aligned with USMLE Step 1 is prioritized and stays with PCCS. Content known or potentially aligned with Step 2 CK goes to CCS. A gap's Step alignment is set from the USMLE content crosswalk of the curriculum map and the current USMLE content outlines.

Routing order:

  1. The USMLE rule decides between PCCS and CCS for content taught in modules and clerkships; when a gap is on both Steps, PCCS leads and CCS covers the clinical application.
  2. The Clinical Competency group (the Director of Clinical Competency, the POM directors and the Clinical Competency Mentors) takes clinical-skills gaps: communication, procedures, POM and PEAKS.
  3. CIS reviews only gaps in AOC or thread structure, and may be consulted on others.
  4. The EEC decides every gap.

USMLE tags are from the 2026 Step 1 and Step 2 CK content outlines and the Oct 16 crosswalk.
